Stud 8 Starting Hands

3rd Street Reference - Kevin Haney

There are no starting hand charts because it is based on exposed cards and action at the table.

You are dealt 3 unpaired low cards 1 of 6 hands dealt.

Almost Always Almost Always Playable Holdings
▼

These hands are almost always playable from any position regardless of exposed cards and action you face.

Trips

  • On 3rd get as much money into the pot as possible.

Two Aces

  • Having low kicker preferred
  • If Aces are split or buried, opponent will have hard time knowing which way we are going.
  • Short handed pots will produce more scoops
  • High kickers has less value and when aces are buried, everyone knows we're going high.
    • May not want to reraise if there are many players in the pot. Can see what develops on 4th and maybe make a snug fold.

3 Suited Low Cards

  • If many of your suit is out, you are playing a one way hand.

3 Wheel Cards w/ Ace

  • Close decision if raise or not when someone completed.
  • Lean towards calling in a low ante game and raising in a high ante game to get people to fold out equity or make bad calls on future streets.

Zero Gappers (No Ace)

  • 5-6-7 and 6-7-8 can become marginal in multiway pots contested by several low cards. But in heads up pots, these do well and you should get aggressive on 3rd street, especially in a high ante game.
  • Two flush 6-7-8 is 50% against pair of kings and 3 Wheel Cards w/ Ace.
  • Can be dangerous to reraise an Ace, but we have around 55% equity against someone who is completing close to 100% of the time.
    • If they have Aces, we have around 43% equity and if the bring in folds 3 low cards we are in better shape.

3 to a 6 w/ Ace

  • Almost always playable even if a few aces are dead, especially if you hold two suit.

3 Wheel Cards One Gapper Without Ace

Often Often Playable Hands
▼

These are good enough to play around 75% or more of the time.

One-Gappers (No Ace)

  • Want your straight draw card to be live.
  • If you have 3 wheel cards or 3 to a 6 these are playable even with your straight outs dead.
  • The higher one gapper gutter outs need to be live, especially if there are many other small cards contesting the pot.
  • In some situations, there may need to pick up a straight draw on 4th to realize equity.
  • 8-6-5 rainbow is a 53% favorite over 2-3-4 rainbow and 49% against A-3-4 rainbow. This is because more chance to make higher pairs.
  • If low card in a steal position will raise 10-4-4 or A-J-4, then a reraise has a lot of value as we are equity favorites.

Three to a 7 or 8 w/ Ace

  • Prefer shorthanded action
  • Play aggressively when ace is up
  • Value goes down when aces are dead.

Kings and Queens

  • High pairs are good if:
    • exposed cards and action suggests we have best high hand
    • Pot doesn't go multiway
  • Playing against many low cards can get rough and we can get jammed on in later streets.
  • Should fold against an Ace that completes from a non steal position.
  • Pair of queens should be folded when a king completes.
  • Sometimes make tight folds when there is an ace up card behind
  • In early position, best to fold when ace and many other low cards left to act.
  • Many opponents with an Ace will reraise many hands that don't currently beat a pair of kings, so only fold to opponents playing straight forward.
  • Very aggressive players will take off with (2-J)A and pick up pair and small draw along the way. Calling down is high variance, but we need to accept this situation.

Low Pair w/ Ace

  • If pair cards are live, hand has lots of value even if you're up against good low draw and a high pair (other than aces) example with A-6-6

Flush Draws w/ 2 Low Cards

  • Multi-way, always important flush outs are very live
  • Want to limit the field.
  • With high card up, can represent a big pair when low fails to improve.
  • When opponents low improves, we can make sneaky lows ourself.
  • With low card up, we should get aggressive to knock out raggedy lows that will steal half of pot.
  • Against high pairs, we've got good odds to improve one way or another.
Sometimes Sometimes Playable Hands
▼

These hands are only playable in certain 3rd street situations when the board is advantageous to us, we can get heads-up against a weak range, or as a potential ante steal.

Marginal hands should make disciplined folds on 3rd or get out early when not improved and it appears opponent has.

Two Gappers No Ace

3 to a 6:

  • Can always call a single complete and join a multi way pot.
  • Trickier when there is a reraise and our key cards are dead.
    • If a five completes and an ace reraises, a hand such as 2-4-6 should probably hit the muck, although calling probably isn't too bad if we have two live suits.

Three to a seven or eight two gapper:

  • Is marginal, especially with highest card exposed.
    • Most of time don't call complete from early position and can frequently open complete if not too many threatening cards behind us.

Low Pair with Low Kicker (no ace)

  • This is a decent hand & will do relatively well in heads up pots. (Two flush and zero gapper)
  • Whether or not to play depends on if our pair outs are live.
  • Get aggressive on 3rd street if pair outs are live.
  • Difficult multiway bc to continue we need to improve and opponents need to brick
  • For a single complete, can enter into a 3 way pot against a probably high hand and low hand bc of implied odds.
    • If you don't improve on 4th, pitch it.
  • 4-7-4 is mediocre should only play when can win uncontested on 3rd or are defending the bring in

Flush Draws w/ 2 or 3 High Cards

  • Drawing hands to half pot need to be very live and have strong potential.
  • 10-Q-K three flush is strong enough to reraise an opponent, if they are not showing an ace.
  • This plays more as a push as want to knock out mediocre lows
  • Since we reraise a pair of kings, we should also do this with a three flush.
  • If you have chance to win uncontested pot on 3rd, your flush outs can afford to be a little dead, especially if you have king up. (Might get queens or jacks to fold)

Jacks, Tens, Nines

  • Should fold in early position
  • Mostly playable as open complete when in middle or later position & not many threatening cards behind.
  • Most all cards left to act are a threat. Hand doesn't play well multiway and when a low bricks, they usually catch an overcard to our pair.
  • Fold to a complete from a higher up pair
  • Even if Ace is betting in a steal spot and still bring in left to act, can be dicey.
  • If our kicker is an Ace or another ace already folded, equity goes up and you should reraise.
  • If hand is buried and have a low card up like (J-J)3 we can assume more risk.

Low Pair w/ Higher Kicker (No Ace)

  • This is marginal.
  • If our kicker is higher than some potential pairs that we run into, hand is good enough to play.
  • Ex. If we have (K-6)6 and a jack, queen, and 3 are left, we can complete. If we get reraised by a pair of jacks or queens, not too bad. Our hand is hidden and we have implied odds.
    • We have hidden trips if we pair our door card. If he pairs his, we should fold

Razz Lows

  • Should not be calling legitimate completes with these hands
  • They are pure stealing hands or defending the bring-in against a wide range.

Steal Holdings

  • These should only be played when we expect to steal successfully or defending the bring in against a late position open.
  • Ace is a powerful up card so we should always consider stealing when we have reasonable hole cards as opponents will tighten up against it and not likely to get reraised.
